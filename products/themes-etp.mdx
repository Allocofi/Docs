## Overview

ETFT pricing separates the value of a reference portfolio from the actual cost of trading it.

Each ETFT therefore uses three related but distinct pricing concepts:

| Pricing Layer | Purpose | Includes Management Fee | Includes Transaction Costs |
| :-- | :-- | :-: | :-: |
| **Indicative NAV (iNAV)** | Tracks the theoretical value of the reference portfolio | No | No |
| **Net Product NAV** | Reflects the value of the ETFT after accrued product-level fees | Yes | No |
| **Executable Price** | Determines the economics of an actual Buy or Sell | Yes | Yes |

This distinction allows investors to separate the performance of the investment idea itself from product-level fees and the costs of entering or exiting the underlying portfolio.

---

## Indicative NAV

The **Indicative NAV (iNAV)** represents the theoretical value of the ETFT's reference portfolio.

It is calculated directly from the defined portfolio weights and market prices of the underlying assets. It does **not** depend on the amount of assets currently held by the ETFT or the number of investors in the product.

In other words, iNAV answers a simple question:

> **How would the reference portfolio have performed if it had been held according to the ETFT methodology?**

The iNAV incorporates relevant corporate actions, including stock splits and dividends, according to the applicable ETFT methodology.

It does not include:

- Management fees
- Brokerage costs
- Buy or sell execution costs
- Bid-ask spreads
- Market impact
- Investor-specific transaction costs

As a result, iNAV should be understood as a **portfolio reference value**, rather than a guaranteed executable price.

---

## Calculating Indicative NAV

At the beginning of each portfolio period, each underlying asset is assigned a target weight.

For a portfolio containing $$n$$ assets:

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n} w_{i,r} = 1
$$

where $$w_{i,r}$$ represents the target weight of asset $$i$$ at the most recent portfolio rebalancing time $$r$$.

The iNAV at time $$t$$ can then be expressed as:

$$
iNAV_t
=
iNAV_r
\times
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
w_{i,r}
\frac{A_{i,t}}{A_{i,r}}
$$

where:

- $$iNAV_r$$ = indicative NAV at the beginning of the portfolio period
- $$w_{i,r}$$ = target weight of asset $$i$$
- $$A_{i,r}$$ = corporate-action-adjusted reference price of asset $$i$$ at the beginning of the period
- $$A_{i,t}$$ = corporate-action-adjusted reference price of asset $$i$$ at time $$t$$

Corporate-action adjustments are used to maintain economic continuity through events such as stock splits and dividends.

## Example — Pelosi ETFT

Consider the Pelosi ETFT.

Its reference portfolio is constructed using predefined weights derived from the applicable portfolio methodology.

For example:

| Ticker | Weight |
| :-- | --: |
| GOOGL | 13.06% |
| AMZN | 13.02% |
| AVGO | 12.49% |
| NVDA | 12.04% |
| AAPL | 11.76% |
| V | 10.89% |
| MSFT | 10.53% |
| CRM | 7.45% |
| CRWD | 3.73% |
| AXP | 1.94% |
| NFLX | 1.62% |
| IBKR | 0.71% |
| CMCSA | 0.43% |
| DBX | 0.32% |

The Pelosi ETFT iNAV is therefore driven by the weighted performance of these underlying positions.

Conceptually:

$$
iNAV_t
=
iNAV_r
\times
\left(
0.1306R_{\mathrm{GOOGL}}
+
0.1302R_{\mathrm{AMZN}}
+
0.1249R_{\mathrm{AVGO}}
+
\cdots
+
0.0032R_{\mathrm{DBX}}
\right)
$$

where:

$$
R_i = \frac{A_{i,t}}{A_{i,r}}
$$

The displayed weights may be normalized to 100% where required due to rounding.

<Note>
  The Pelosi ETFT iNAV represents the performance of the reference portfolio methodology. It does not imply that the displayed iNAV is calculated from the market value of assets already held in a brokerage account.
</Note>

## Portfolio Rebalancing

When an ETFT methodology produces a new set of portfolio weights, the new allocation becomes effective from the applicable rebalancing point.

The iNAV is **chain-linked** across portfolio periods so that a change in target weights does not, by itself, create an artificial gain or loss.

At the rebalancing point:

$$
iNAV_{r}^{+} = iNAV_{r}^{-}
$$

The new weights are then used to calculate subsequent portfolio performance.

This allows the historical iNAV series to represent a continuous strategy while the underlying portfolio composition evolves over time.

---

## Corporate Actions

Corporate actions are incorporated into the iNAV methodology to preserve the economic value of the reference portfolio.

These may include:

- Cash dividends
- Stock dividends
- Stock splits and reverse splits
- Mergers and acquisitions
- Spin-offs
- Delistings
- Other material corporate actions

For example, a stock split changes the number of shares represented by a position but should not, by itself, create a gain or loss in the ETFT's iNAV.

Similarly, dividends are reflected according to the applicable ETFT methodology so that portfolio performance captures the relevant economic return of the underlying position.

## From iNAV to Net Product NAV

The public iNAV is a **gross reference value** and does not include the ETFT management fee.

For an investable ETFT, the applicable management fee is accrued separately and reflected in the product's net economics.

Conceptually:

$$
NAV_t^{net}
=
iNAV_t \times F_t
$$

where:

- $$iNAV_t$$ = gross indicative NAV
- $$F_t$$ = cumulative management-fee adjustment factor
- $$NAV_t^{net}$$ = product value after accrued management fees

For a management fee of $$m$$ per annum, a daily accrual can conceptually be represented as:

$$
F_t
=
\prod_{d=1}^{D}
\left(1-\frac{m}{365}\right)
$$

where $$D$$ represents the number of fee-accrual days.

The applicable management fee varies by ETFT and is disclosed on the relevant product page.

<Info>
  Management fees affect the economics of the investable ETFT but are intentionally excluded from the public iNAV so that the reference portfolio performance can be observed independently of product fees.
</Info>

## Executable Pricing

The **Net Product NAV is not necessarily the price at which an investor can buy or sell an ETFT**.

When an investor trades an ETFT, Alloco must execute the corresponding underlying portfolio through its integrated brokerage infrastructure.

Actual transaction economics therefore depend on real market execution.

The relationship can be summarized as:

$$
Reference\ Portfolio
\rightarrow
iNAV
\rightarrow
Net\ Product\ NAV
\rightarrow
Executable\ Buy/Sell\ Price
$$

---

## Buy Pricing

When a user enters an amount of USDC to buy an ETFT, Alloco generates an executable quote based on current market conditions.

The estimated buy price can be represented conceptually as:

$$
P_t^{buy}
=
NAV_t^{net}
+
C_t^{buy}
$$

where:

- $$NAV_t^{net}$$ = applicable Net Product NAV
- $$C_t^{buy}$$ = incremental cost of establishing the corresponding underlying positions

Buy-side transaction costs may reflect:

- Brokerage and execution costs
- Bid-ask spreads
- Market impact
- Price movement during execution
- Applicable regulatory or transaction charges
- Required currency or stablecoin conversion costs, where applicable

There is no separate **Buy Fee** charged by Alloco.

Instead, applicable costs associated with establishing the underlying portfolio are reflected through the execution pricing process.

### Minimum ETFT Received

For a purchase amount $$U$$ denominated in USDC:

$$
Q_{\min}
=
\frac{U}{P_{\max}^{buy}}
$$

where:

- $$U$$ = USDC amount entered by the investor
- $$P_{\max}^{buy}$$ = maximum execution price allowed by the applicable quote
- $$Q_{\min}$$ = minimum amount of ETFT expected to be received

This is why the Alloco interface displays:

> **Minimum ETFT Received**

rather than treating the displayed iNAV as a guaranteed transaction price.

---

## Sell Pricing

Selling an ETFT follows the same mechanism in reverse.

The corresponding portion of the underlying portfolio is sold through the integrated brokerage infrastructure.

The executable sell value can be represented conceptually as:

$$
P_t^{sell}
=
NAV_t^{net}
-
C_t^{sell}
-
F_t^{sell}
$$

where:

- $$NAV_t^{net}$$ = applicable Net Product NAV
- $$C_t^{sell}$$ = costs associated with liquidating the underlying portfolio
- $$F_t^{sell}$$ = applicable ETFT sell fee

### Minimum USDC Received

For an investor selling $$Q$$ ETFT tokens:

$$
U_{\min}
=
Q \times P_{\min}^{sell}
$$

where:

- $$Q$$ = ETFT amount being sold
- $$P_{\min}^{sell}$$ = minimum executable sell price defined by the quote
- $$U_{\min}$$ = minimum amount of USDC expected to be received

The interface therefore displays:

> **Minimum USDC Received**

before the investor confirms the transaction.

---

## Why Buy and Sell Prices May Differ

At the same point in time:

$$
P_t^{buy}
\geq
NAV_t^{net}
\geq
P_t^{sell}
$$

under normal market conditions.

This difference does not necessarily represent an additional fee charged by Alloco.

It can arise because buying the underlying portfolio incurs different execution economics from selling it.

The difference may reflect:

- Bid-ask spreads
- Brokerage costs
- Market liquidity
- Market impact
- Extended-hours conditions
- Underlying price movement during execution
- Portfolio size and composition

As a result, two investors trading the same ETFT at the same time but in different directions may receive different executable prices.

---

## Quote vs. Final Execution

A quote is generated **before** the underlying portfolio has been fully executed.

The quote uses current market information to determine the applicable minimum output and execution parameters.

After the investor confirms the transaction:

1. The order enters **Processing**
2. The corresponding underlying assets are submitted for execution
3. Actual execution prices and applicable transaction costs are determined
4. The final ETFT or USDC amount is calculated
5. The order becomes **Claimable**
6. Once claimed, the order becomes **Completed**

<Warning>
  The iNAV is not an offer or guaranteed executable price. Actual execution depends on prevailing market prices, available liquidity, spreads, market conditions, and the successful execution of the underlying portfolio.
</Warning>

| **Bid-ask spread / execution impact** | — | — | ✓ | ✓ |
