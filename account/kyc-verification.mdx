## Overview

Launching an ETFT is a structured process that connects investment design, portfolio methodology, execution infrastructure, onchain issuance, and ongoing product operations.

The exact launch timeline depends on product complexity, asset availability, required integrations, legal review, and distribution requirements.

## Step 1 — Define the Investment Idea

Start by defining what the ETFT is intended to represent.

The initial product brief should include:

- Product name and proposed ticker
- Investment thesis
- Product type
- Target asset universe
- Portfolio objective
- Intended investor or distribution audience
- Reference portfolio, strategy, or data source

For example, a public-insight ETFT may begin with the objective of translating disclosed investment activity into a rules-based equity portfolio.

## Step 2 — Configure the Portfolio

Define how the investment idea becomes a portfolio.

Core portfolio parameters include:

- Eligible underlying assets
- Target weights
- Weighting methodology
- Maximum or minimum position limits
- Cash treatment
- Rebalancing rules
- Data sources
- Corporate-action treatment

For a fixed-weight portfolio:

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i = 1
$$

where $$w_i$$ represents the target weight of each underlying asset.

Portfolio rules should be sufficiently clear to reproduce the intended exposure consistently.

## Step 3 — Define Product Rules

Once the portfolio methodology is established, define how the investable ETFT should operate.

Typical parameters include:

| Parameter | Example |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Trading Asset** | USDC |
| **NAV Currency** | USD |
| **Trading Model** | Real-time Buy / Sell |
| **Management Fee** | Product-specific |
| **Sell Fee** | Product-specific |
| **Supported Networks** | Ethereum / BNB Chain |
| **Rebalancing** | Calendar / Event / Drift based |
| **Execution Venue** | Supported integrated brokerage infrastructure |

Product-specific rules are documented before launch and should remain consistent with the applicable product methodology and disclosures.

## Step 4 — Feasibility Review

Alloco reviews whether the proposed ETFT can be operated as designed.

The review may include:

### Asset & Execution

- Asset availability
- Fractional trading support
- Market liquidity
- Trading hours
- Order-size constraints
- Corporate actions
- Market or asset restrictions

### Data

- Market data availability
- Historical price data
- Corporate-action data
- Strategy or signal data
- Licensing and permitted use

### Product Operations

- NAV methodology
- Rebalancing feasibility
- Execution workflow
- Settlement workflow
- Smart contract requirements

### Legal & Distribution

- Proposed issuer and product structure
- Target jurisdictions
- Investor eligibility
- Marketing and distribution constraints
- Required product disclosures

A proposed ETFT may require changes to its portfolio or operating rules before it can proceed to build.

## Step 5 — Structure & Deploy

After the product passes feasibility review, Alloco configures the infrastructure required to operate the ETFT.

This may include:

- ETFT product configuration
- Portfolio and weighting logic
- iNAV calculation
- Buy and Sell quote logic
- Brokerage execution workflow
- Fee configuration
- Smart contract deployment
- Supported network configuration
- Claim and settlement logic
- Reporting infrastructure

The resulting structure connects the creator's methodology with the infrastructure required for real-world execution and onchain settlement.

## Step 6 — Test & Validate

Before launch, the product is tested across its complete lifecycle.

Typical validation includes:

**Pricing**

- Indicative NAV calculation
- Corporate-action adjustments
- Buy quotes
- Sell quotes
- Fee calculations

**Trading**

- Underlying basket generation
- Brokerage order routing
- Partial or failed execution handling
- Execution reconciliation

**Onchain**

- Wallet interactions
- Buy confirmation
- Claim flow
- Sell confirmation
- USDC settlement

The core transaction path should be validated end-to-end:

> **Quote → Processing → Claimable → Completed**

## Step 7 — Launch

Once testing and required product reviews are complete, the ETFT can be enabled for its approved distribution channels.

At launch, investors should be able to access the applicable:

- Product overview
- Holdings and weights
- Methodology
- Indicative NAV
- Fees
- Risk disclosures
- Legal documentation
- Buy and Sell interface

Launch does not end the product lifecycle. The ETFT then enters ongoing monitoring and management.

See **Lifecycle Management** for post-launch operations.

## Launch Checklist

| Area | Required Before Launch |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Portfolio** | Assets, weights, methodology, rebalancing rules |
| **Pricing** | iNAV and executable pricing validated |
| **Execution** | Underlying execution workflow tested |
| **Smart Contract** | Supported network deployment validated |
| **Settlement** | Buy, Sell, and Claim flows tested |
| **Disclosure** | Product terms, fees, risks, and methodology available |
| **Distribution** | Eligibility and distribution requirements confirmed |
