SURE is designed to obtain its principal investment exposure through the Trivesta Global Allocation Fund. The underlying sub-fund has a broad global mandate and may invest across traditional and alternative assets, while current product materials emphasize liquid foreign-exchange markets and precious metals, including gold.

<Info>
  The strategy is not fully described by a single formula or trading signal in the supplied documents. This page summarizes the disclosed mandate and operating framework without representing undisclosed models, positions or guarantees.
</Info>

## Investment Objective

The underlying fund's stated objective is to seek **long-term income and capital appreciation**.

Its permitted investment universe includes, among other things:

- debt securities and obligations, including below-investment-grade instruments;
- funds of funds, private funds, hedge funds and private-equity funds;
- listed and unlisted equities and equity-related instruments;
- structured products;
- mutual funds, unit trusts, REITs and ETFs;
- currencies and commodities;
- futures, options, warrants and swaps;
- exchange-traded and over-the-counter derivatives;
- real estate, private projects and private companies; and
- cash, cash equivalents and money-market funds.

The investment manager may use short selling, margin trading, hedging, borrowing and other investment techniques permitted by the governing documents.

## Strategy Implementation Framework

### 1. Opportunity Selection

The investment team identifies opportunities across currencies, gold and other permitted markets. Selection may consider:

- liquidity and executable depth;
- volatility and market regime;
- macroeconomic and policy conditions;
- risk-adjusted return potential;
- correlation with existing positions;
- financing, carry and transaction costs; and
- available margin and counterparty capacity.

### 2. Position Construction

The strategy may use:

- long and short positions;
- spot, forwards, futures and options;
- directional, relative-value or hedging positions;
- margin and collateralized trading;
- allocations to funds or structured products; and
- cash or cash equivalents when risk or opportunity conditions are unattractive.

### 3. Active Risk Management

The supplied materials describe a framework emphasizing:

- predefined risk parameters;
- active monitoring and position management;
- volatility control;
- drawdown management;
- systematic execution;
- adjustment to changing market conditions; and
- the ability to close all or part of the portfolio and retain cash with the custodian.

### 4. Portfolio Adaptation

The investment manager may change the strategy in response to market conditions and is not necessarily required to notify shareholders of every strategy adjustment, subject to the governing documents and applicable law.

<Warning>
  The flexibility of the mandate allows the strategy to adapt, but it also creates mandate, model, leverage, liquidity and valuation risk. SURE should not be described as exposure only to a fixed basket of five instruments.
</Warning>

## Use of Leverage and Derivatives

The Appendix states that the sub-fund may employ leverage through borrowing, structured notes, repurchase agreements, margin trading and derivatives.

It describes an expected leverage level of up to **150% of NAV**, but also states that:

- the expected level may be exceeded in certain circumstances; and
- there is no absolute limit on the amount of leverage that may be employed.

Accordingly, 150% should not be presented as a guaranteed hard cap.

Leverage can:

- magnify gains and losses;
- increase margin and collateral requirements;
- force liquidation at unfavorable prices;
- create nonlinear derivative exposure;
- increase counterparty and financing risk; and
- cause losses even when the investment thesis is ultimately correct.

## Distribution and Performance-Fee Design

The underlying fund's three classes have annualized monthly distribution objectives of 8%, 9% and 10%.

The performance-fee mechanism is economically important:

1. each class has a corresponding simple hard hurdle of 8%, 9% or 10%;
2. performance is assessed against a high-water mark;
3. fees and expenses and distributions are taken into account under the Appendix methodology; and
4. the investment manager is entitled to **100% of net new profit above the applicable hurdle and high-water mark**.

<Info>
  This structure is designed to direct performance above the relevant hurdle to the investment manager as a performance fee. It effectively caps the ordinary holder's participation in upside above the applicable hurdle, while the holder remains exposed to downside.
</Info>

## Relationship Between Returns, Distributions and NAV

A monthly distribution does not establish that the strategy earned the same amount during that month.

Depending on performance and the governing documents:

- a distribution may be funded from income or gains;
- a distribution may reduce NAV;
- unrealized gains may reverse;
- losses may be borne by investor capital;
- fees may accrue on unrealized values; and
- distributions may be changed or suspended.

SURE performance is therefore determined by both distributions received and changes in SURE NAV.

## Cash and Liquidity Management

The underlying fund may hold cash or cash equivalents:

- pending investment;
- as collateral;
- to manage market risk;
- to support redemptions;
- when the investment manager closes positions; or
- when market conditions are unfavorable.

Holding cash can reduce market exposure but may also create performance drag and bank or custodian concentration.
