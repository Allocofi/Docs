The underlying Trivesta Fund follows a systematic **foreign-exchange statistical arbitrage strategy** and supports SURE's single fixed annual distribution rate of **9%**.

Rather than relying primarily on whether one currency rises or falls, the strategy seeks to capture recurring pricing dislocations between currency pairs that demonstrate statistically meaningful relationships.

The strategy has three principal components:

<Info>
  Statistical arbitrage describes a probabilistic investment process, not risk-free arbitrage. Historical relationships may change, fail to converge or remain dislocated for longer than expected.

## 1. Relationship Modelling

The strategy evaluates historical and current FX data to identify currency pairs or groups with stable and economically meaningful relationships.

Inputs may include:

- spot and forward prices;
- bid-ask spreads;
- rolling returns;
- realized volatility;
- correlation and covariance;
- interest-rate differentials;
- liquidity and market depth;
- execution costs; and
- macroeconomic-event risk.

Possible statistical methods may include:

- correlation analysis;
- cointegration testing;
- spread and ratio analysis;
- standardized-deviation signals;
- volatility-adjusted thresholds;
- regime classification; and
- cross-sectional ranking.

### Standardized Spread

A simplified standardized spread may be expressed as:

$$
z_t
=
\frac{
S_t-\mu_t
}{
\sigma_t
}
$$

| Term | Meaning |
| :-- | :-- |
| $$S_t$$ | Current observed currency spread |
| $$\mu_t$$ | Estimated mean of the spread |
| $$\sigma_t$$ | Estimated standard deviation of the spread |
| $$z_t$$ | Distance of the current spread from its estimated mean |

A large positive or negative value may indicate that the relationship is unusually far from its expected range. A signal is not executed solely because a threshold is crossed; liquidity, costs, volatility, event risk and portfolio exposure must also be considered.

## 2. Relative-Value Position Construction

When a statistical relationship becomes sufficiently dislocated, the strategy may:

1. take a long position in one currency pair;
2. take an offsetting short position in a related currency pair;
3. size both legs using the estimated hedge ratio;
4. reduce unwanted exposure to individual currencies; and
5. seek to close the position as the spread normalizes.

A simplified relative-value position can be represented as:

$$
\Pi_{\text{pair},t}
=
w_A R_{A,t}
-
w_B R_{B,t}
-
C_{\text{trading},t}
-
C_{\text{financing},t}
$$

| Term | Meaning |
| :-- | :-- |
| $$w_A$$ | Long exposure weight |
| $$w_B$$ | Short exposure weight |
| $$R_{A,t}$$ | Return of the long FX leg |
| $$R_{B,t}$$ | Return of the short FX leg |
| Trading costs | Bid-ask spread, commission and slippage |
| Financing costs | Swap, carry, margin and funding costs |

### Illustrative Position

Assume the model identifies an unusual divergence between two related FX instruments:

| Position | Exposure | Objective |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| FX Instrument A | Long | Benefit if the undervalued leg strengthens |
| FX Instrument B | Short | Hedge shared market exposure and benefit if the overvalued leg weakens |
| Combined Position | Relative-value spread | Seek return as the relationship normalizes |

The purpose of the position is to isolate the relative pricing difference. The hedge may be imperfect and residual directional exposure may remain.

## Portfolio Construction

The strategy does not allocate solely to the trade with the largest headline deviation. Each position is evaluated together with expected return, volatility, correlation, liquidity, leverage, margin and concentration.

A simplified optimization objective is:

$$
\max_{w}
\left[
\mu^\top w
-
\lambda_{\text{risk}} w^\top \Sigma w
-
\lambda_{\text{cost}} C(w,w_0)
-
\lambda_{\text{concentration}} P(w)
\right]
$$

The target portfolio may be subject to constraints such as:

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\text{Net FX Exposure}| &\leq E_{\max} \\
\text{Gross Leverage} &\leq L_{\max} \\
w_i &\leq w_{i,\max} \\
\text{Currency Concentration}_j &\leq C_{j,\max} \\
\text{Margin Utilization} &\leq M_{\max}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Signal-to-Execution Process

<Steps>
  <Step title="Observe">
    Collect FX prices, spreads, volatility, liquidity, rates, trading costs and event-risk information.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Normalize">
    Align quote conventions, timestamps, currency exposures, contract terms and data quality.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Estimate">
    Calculate the expected relationship, current deviation, mean-reversion probability, costs and downside risk.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Optimize">
    Select position sizes subject to exposure, leverage, concentration, liquidity and margin limits.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Execute">
    Coordinate the long and short legs using price, slippage and order-size controls.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Monitor and Rebalance">
    Track spread convergence, residual FX exposure, volatility, margin and model stability; then close or resize positions when required.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Trade Admission Rule

An opportunity is eligible for execution only when its expected net return exceeds its risk-adjusted threshold:

$$
\mathbb{E}[\Pi_{\text{net}}]
=
\mathbb{E}[\Pi_{\text{spread convergence}}]
+
\mathbb{E}[\Pi_{\text{carry}}]
-
\widehat{C}_{\text{trading}}
-
\widehat{C}_{\text{slippage}}
-
\widehat{C}_{\text{financing}}
-
\widehat{C}_{\text{risk}}
> 0
$$

The strategy may require an additional safety buffer to account for model uncertainty and changing market conditions.

## Sources of Strategy P&L

The strategy's aggregate net result can be summarized as:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Pi_{\text{net}}
=\;&
\Pi_{\text{spread convergence}}
+
\Pi_{\text{relative-value movement}}
+
\Pi_{\text{carry}} \\
&-
C_{\text{trading}}
-
C_{\text{slippage}}
-
C_{\text{financing}}
-
C_{\text{operational}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Positive statistical signals do not necessarily produce positive net returns. Profitability depends on convergence, execution quality, transaction costs, model stability, liquidity and risk management.

## What Can Cause Losses?

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Relationship Breakdown" icon="link-slash">
    A historical relationship may weaken, disappear or permanently change after the position is established.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Delayed or Failed Convergence" icon="arrows-left-right-to-line">
    The spread may continue widening or remain dislocated for longer than the strategy can hold the position.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Execution and Leg Risk" icon="arrows-split-up-and-left">
    One leg may execute while the offsetting leg is delayed, rejected or partially filled, creating temporary directional exposure.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Liquidity and Market Impact" icon="droplet">
    Available liquidity may deteriorate, widening spreads and increasing the cost of entering, rebalancing or closing positions.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Leverage and Margin Risk" icon="triangle-exclamation">
    Leverage may magnify a small model error and create margin calls or forced liquidation before the spread converges.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Crowded Position Unwind" icon="people-group">
    Other market participants may hold similar positions and exit at the same time, causing rapid spread widening.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Counterparty Risk" icon="building-columns">
    A bank, broker or liquidity provider may experience insolvency, trading restrictions, operational failure or legal intervention.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Model and Data Risk" icon="microchip">
    Market data may be delayed or incorrect, and estimated correlations, volatility, costs or hedge ratios may differ from realized outcomes.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
