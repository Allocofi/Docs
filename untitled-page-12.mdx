## Overview

Launching an ETFT is the beginning of its operational lifecycle.

After launch, each product requires ongoing portfolio monitoring, pricing, rebalancing, corporate-action handling, execution oversight, reporting, and product maintenance.

> **Monitor → Rebalance → Execute → Reconcile → Report**

## Portfolio Monitoring

The ETFT portfolio is monitored against its applicable methodology and product rules.

Monitoring may include:

- Current portfolio weights
- Target portfolio weights
- Portfolio drift
- Asset eligibility
- Market liquidity
- Data availability
- Corporate actions
- Trading restrictions
- Product-specific risk limits

The purpose of monitoring is to identify events requiring a portfolio or operational response.

## Portfolio Rebalancing

An ETFT may use one or more rebalancing methods.

### Calendar-Based Rebalancing

The portfolio is reviewed or adjusted according to a predefined schedule.

### Event-Driven Rebalancing

A defined external event or methodology signal triggers a portfolio update.

For example, a public-insight ETFT may update after relevant information becomes available and is incorporated according to the product methodology.

### Drift-Based Rebalancing

The portfolio may rebalance when actual or reference weights move outside predefined tolerances.

### Methodology-Driven Rebalancing

Custom strategies may define their own objective triggers, provided those rules can be implemented and disclosed consistently.

## Rebalancing Execution

When a rebalance is required, the new target portfolio is translated into the underlying trades needed to move from the existing exposure toward the updated allocation.

The process generally includes:

1. Determine the new target portfolio.
2. Calculate the difference from the existing portfolio.
3. Generate required Buy and Sell orders.
4. Route the underlying orders for execution.
5. Reconcile completed executions.
6. Update portfolio records and reporting.

Actual portfolio weights may temporarily differ from target weights because of execution timing, market movement, liquidity, or other market conditions.

## Corporate Actions

Corporate actions affecting underlying securities are handled according to the applicable ETFT methodology.

Relevant events may include:

- Dividends
- Stock splits and reverse splits
- Mergers and acquisitions
- Spin-offs
- Tender offers
- Delistings
- Symbol changes

Corporate actions may require adjustments to prices, quantities, portfolio composition, or methodology treatment.

The objective is to preserve economic continuity where reasonably possible while reflecting the actual event affecting the underlying asset.

## NAV & Pricing Maintenance

The ETFT pricing infrastructure continues to maintain:

- Indicative NAV
- Corporate-action adjustments
- Management-fee accrual
- Buy quotes
- Sell quotes
- Transaction-level execution adjustments

The public iNAV remains a reference value based on the portfolio methodology, while executable pricing reflects actual transaction conditions.

See **Pricing & NAV** for the detailed pricing framework.

## Methodology Updates

From time to time, a product methodology may require modification.

Potential changes include:

- Adding or removing eligible assets
- Changing weighting rules
- Updating rebalancing triggers
- Changing a data source
- Updating corporate-action treatment
- Modifying product risk controls

Material methodology changes should be reviewed before implementation and may require updated product documentation or investor communication.

Historical methodology changes should not be used to retroactively rewrite previously published investment performance unless explicitly required by the applicable methodology or correction policy.

## Fee Changes

Changes to management fees or other product-level fees should be handled according to the applicable product terms and disclosure requirements.

A fee change may require:

- Product configuration updates
- Updated documentation
- Advance notice, where applicable
- Legal or distribution review

Transaction-level brokerage or market execution costs may change independently of product-level fees because they depend on external execution conditions.

## Product Suspension

Trading or product operations may need to be temporarily restricted under exceptional circumstances.

Potential reasons include:

- Underlying market closure
- Trading halts
- Insufficient market liquidity
- Broker or execution interruption
- Material pricing or data issues
- Blockchain disruption
- Smart contract incident
- Legal or regulatory requirement

A suspension is intended to protect the integrity of pricing, execution, or settlement and does not guarantee that losses can be prevented.

## Product Termination

An ETFT may eventually be discontinued according to its applicable product terms.

A termination process may include:

1. Suspend new Buy activity.
2. Notify affected users where required.
3. Determine the final portfolio treatment.
4. Liquidate applicable underlying positions where required.
5. Determine final settlement amounts.
6. Make assets available for Claim.
7. Complete outstanding settlement and close the product.

The exact termination process depends on the legal and operational structure of the ETFT.

## Reporting

Lifecycle reporting may include:

- Current holdings and weights
- Indicative NAV
- Historical performance
- Rebalancing history
- Methodology updates
- Fee information
- Material corporate actions
- Product status

The level and frequency of reporting may vary depending on the ETFT and applicable product requirements.
