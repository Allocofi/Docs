## Overview

Product configuration converts an investment idea into a precise set of rules that can be priced, executed, monitored, and operated as an ETFT.

The objective is to make the methodology sufficiently clear that portfolio construction and lifecycle events can be handled consistently.

## Core Configuration

Each ETFT begins with a product specification.

| Configuration | Description | Example |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Product Name** | Public name of the ETFT | Pelosi ETFT |
| **Symbol** | Product ticker | \[Ticker\] |
| **Product Type** | Classification of the methodology | KOL / Public Insight ETFT |
| **Asset Universe** | Assets eligible for inclusion | U.S.-listed equities |
| **Base Currency** | Currency used for portfolio valuation | USD |
| **Trading Asset** | Asset used for Buy and Sell settlement | USDC |
| **Trading Model** | Investor transaction model | Real-time Buy / Sell |
| **Supported Networks** | Networks on which the ETFT is available | Ethereum / BNB Chain |
| **Management Fee** | Annual product management fee | Product-specific |
| **Sell Fee** | Applicable sell fee methodology | Product-specific |
| **Rebalancing** | Method used to update portfolio exposure | Event / Calendar / Drift |

## Asset Universe

The asset universe defines which instruments may be included in the ETFT.

Asset eligibility depends on factors including:

- Brokerage availability
- Market liquidity
- Fractional trading support
- Market data coverage
- Trading-hour availability
- Corporate-action support
- Applicable legal and operational restrictions

An asset being publicly traded does not automatically make it eligible for every ETFT.

## Portfolio Weights

Each portfolio assigns a target weight to its underlying assets.

For a fully allocated portfolio:

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i = 1
$$

where $$w_i$$ is the target weight of asset $$i$$.

Weighting methodologies may include:

- Fixed weights
- Equal weights
- Market-cap-based weights
- Signal-based weights
- Score-based weights
- Risk-adjusted weights
- Custom methodology-defined weights

The methodology should also define how rounding, cash, unavailable assets, and temporary restrictions are handled.

## Rebalancing Rules

Rebalancing determines when and how the portfolio returns to its intended allocation.

### Calendar-Based

The portfolio is reviewed or rebalanced at predefined intervals, such as monthly or quarterly.

### Event-Driven

A portfolio update is triggered by a defined event, such as a new public disclosure or methodology signal.

### Drift-Based

Rebalancing occurs when one or more holdings move outside predefined allocation tolerances.

### Hybrid

An ETFT may combine multiple conditions, subject to the product methodology.

The methodology should define the effective time of a portfolio update and how the iNAV series is chain-linked across rebalancing events.

## Pricing Configuration

Each ETFT requires rules for both reference valuation and executable transactions.

The product configuration should define:

- Indicative NAV methodology
- Corporate-action adjustments
- Management-fee accrual
- Buy quote methodology
- Sell quote methodology
- Execution-cost treatment
- Quote validity and execution parameters

The **Indicative NAV (iNAV)** represents the theoretical value of the reference portfolio and should remain distinct from the actual executable Buy or Sell price.

See **Pricing & NAV** in the Onchain ETFT documentation for the full methodology.

## Fee Configuration

Product fees are specified before launch and disclosed on the applicable ETFT product page.

Configurable fee components may include:

- Management fee
- Sell fee
- Holding-period-based fee rules

Underlying brokerage, spread, slippage, market impact, and other execution-level costs are treated separately from Alloco product fees and reflected through executable transaction pricing where applicable.

## Corporate Actions

The configuration must define how corporate actions affecting underlying assets are handled.

These may include:

- Cash dividends
- Stock dividends
- Stock splits and reverse splits
- Mergers and acquisitions
- Spin-offs
- Tender offers
- Delistings

Corporate-action treatment should preserve the economic continuity of the portfolio where reasonably possible while following the applicable methodology.

## Risk Controls

Depending on the ETFT, product-level controls may include:

- Maximum single-asset weight
- Maximum sector concentration
- Minimum liquidity requirements
- Eligible-security rules
- Rebalancing tolerances
- Restricted-asset rules
- Trading suspension conditions
- Data-quality requirements

Risk controls reduce specific operational or portfolio risks but do not eliminate investment loss.

## Methodology Changes

Material changes to an ETFT methodology should be documented and reviewed before becoming effective.

Depending on the nature of the change, updates may require:

- Product configuration changes
- New testing
- Updated disclosures
- Smart contract or infrastructure changes
- Investor communication
- Legal or distribution review

The applicable product documentation should identify the methodology currently in effect.
